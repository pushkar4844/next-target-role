3In detail

Customer-facing roles at Google
Customer Engineer (CE), 3 typesPlatform CE: generalist across infra modernization and AI/ML. Specialist CE: one area (infra modernization, SAP, migrations, AI/ML). FDE / AI roles: hands-on with AI workloads. All are tied to pre-sales: you work with sales (the FSR, Field Sales Rep), marketing and partners.
SegmentsSMB: many accounts, scaled motion, no fixed list. Enterprise / Corp / Select: attached to an FSR, top ~50–100 accounts, all their workloads (e.g. migrating from AWS to GCP).
What you actually doEnd-to-end technical account work with the FSR: workload and technical specs, demos (often built from scratch), workshops, bringing in Google's Professional Services Organization (PSO) or partners for implementation, partner enablement.
Internal cloud architectsA different role. They build demo environments, assets and best practices inside Google; not live on customer environments. Professional Services (PSO) is customer-facing delivery.
Titles to search"Customer Engineer", "Solutions Architect", "Cloud Architect" (also "Solution Engineering"); wording varies by team. AWS L5 = Google L4 (IC4).
Interview process
OrderRecruiter screen → then GCA or technical first, depending on the hiring manager. A strong first technical round can replace the second. GCA and Googleyness are one round each and mandatory. The last round is the presentation.
Role-Related KnowledgeCloud fundamentals and situation questions: full on-prem → cloud journey (plan, scope…); customer wants to host in China but there's no Google region; architecture of an Instagram-like app and how to scale it globally. Plus how agentic AI fits, even for infra modernization roles.
GoogleynessBehavioral and situational, answered in STAR. Example: "A dashboard was built for the sales team but they aren't using it. How would you drive adoption?" They test your thinking process, not technology.
PresentationPanel of 3–4. You get a problem statement and present a solution to them.
GCP knowledgeNot asked directly; interviews are cloud-agnostic. Know the GCP names for what you do on AWS, which database fits (e.g. "SQL vs Postgres, which one and how do you scale?"), and how storage options differ. Show your thought process and ask follow-up questions.
Market noteMigrations are no longer just on-prem → cloud. They are now "migration plus optimization". Read up on recent changes before interviews.
Agentic AI: what gets asked
The list she gaveADK; MCP: how to use it and connect to an MCP server; A2A protocol; how to host an agent; the 4 stages: develop, build, scale, govern (security for govern, user base and management for scale); evals: how you measure an agent beyond human-in-the-loop.
Concept questionsWhy use MCP instead of a normal API, and what's underneath MCP? The shift from prompt engineering to "harness engineering"; graph-based vs loop-based agents; System 1 (fast decision) vs System 2 (reasoning) models.
Culture
No stack rankingYear-end ratings exist; "significant impact" is achievable. Layoffs follow business priorities (currently AI first), not a quarterly stack.
20% projectsSpend 20% of your time with another team (e.g. as an AI/ML specialist) to learn and test a future move.
Her career viewWith tools like Claude Code, tech stack matters less than knowing whether the AI-written code is right. She'd invest in security (infra and data) and data pipelines and cleaning. Prefers Google for growth: horizontal and vertical moves, 20% projects.
