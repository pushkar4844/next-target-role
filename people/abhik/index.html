3In detail

Where the industry is going
Move to in-house GPUsCompanies are moving from external providers to their own GPUs for availability and cost. Texas is "the capital of data centers" right now.
Open models + routingMany companies use Anthropic/OpenAI today, but decent open models exist (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM). Pattern being evaluated: reserved/on-prem capacity first, semantic routing, then fall back to the API. Teams also collect traces (e.g. LangSmith) to fine-tune their own models. Still early and driven by cost and volume.
SupplyGPUs are scarce and chaotic now, likely for ~6 months; memory makers are earning a lot. GPUs get more expensive over time, so buying or long contracts make sense, unlike cars or CPUs that depreciate ("like gold").
Capex now, jobs nextThe billions you see are capex: land and GPUs still being built. Roles open once the infra is half-built; by end-2027 a lot will be ready to operate.
NeocloudsGPU-only clouds such as CoreWeave, TensorWave and Together AI; ~115 in the US (his estimate), hiring heavily. Big companies building their own capacity is why CoreWeave's stock dipped. Many data centers sit outside big cities around DFW and want local talent; one he spoke to outsourced network engineering to Italy.
Why AWS isn't the leader for trainingGPU topology matters: two GPUs physically close are far faster than far-apart ones, and AWS's allocation historically didn't care. That shows up in ClusterMAX ratings.
What the job is
Why it paysAn NVL72 rack costs $3–4M; 72 GPUs at ~$4/GPU-hour is a big hourly bill. Companies pay a lot to keep it running smoothly, recover fast and maximize utilization. CPUs break every 6–12 months; dense GPUs break far more often.
Daily problemsWhere things stall and break. Example: six GPUs synchronizing, one slower, so all wait. ML engineers say "I deployed, the network is saturated"; DevOps says "that's your app." Someone who can own both is missing.
Two kinds of rolesIn-house (bank or large corp): an AI infra team that AI engineers go through. You decide how to deploy on many machines, topology, bottlenecks, time-slicing, quotas. You're the architect. Neoclouds: consulting for smaller companies plus running their own fleet.
Gold standard for on-premMaximum tokens per second, kept continuously, and fault tolerance when something fails. Without NVL72, GPUs talk over InfiniBand, so learn what InfiniBand is and gang scheduling.
Configs are the valueDevelopers can write configs but rarely understand their impact or bottlenecks. Neoclouds and hyperscalers lack this know-how and are pushed for better utilization and faster recovery.
How to learn
Access is the gatekeeperWithout machines it's a chicken-and-egg problem. NVIDIA runs upskilling and certifications.
Text over videoSubstack has many free follow-alongs; YouTube is slower because detailed videos have small audiences. He uses ChatGPT/Codex deep research to find Substack articles, with detailed prompts. He named a woman who teaches a Maven course whose articles are "pretty good". From the description this is very likely Abi Aryan.
PaceLearn concepts close to what you know first; jumping into GPU internals burns you out. He shared his own "AI infra upskilling roadmap" link in chat (orchestration and workload management with Slurm, advanced networking) and offered a call summary by email.
