1Key points

1. Stack skills; don't stay a niche specialist.Under 10 years, combine OS fundamentals + one programming language + one cloud, deep enough to say "L200–L300" on each. Mainframe is valuable but niche. Migrations are being sped up by automation, so being "the mainframe person" alone isn't an advantage.
2. Use agents to work faster.He built reusable agent templates (e.g. one that only writes Terraform) and cut customer demo prep from 6–8 hours to 1–1.5 hours, about 80% less. Use the saved time to keep learning.
3. The interview tests concepts, not GCP.Any one cloud is fine if you can explain the fundamentals: when and which load balancer, cache, database, and the cost vs reliability trade-off.
4. Write your stories before the interview.Audit your last 1–2 years, personal and professional. Write every scenario down with details and numbers. One story can answer 4–5 questions.
5. Pick the manager and team, not the company.Ask for a coffee chat with the hiring manager before accepting.

3In detail

Career strategy
Skill stackHis own path: OS (Linux, Windows) and some databases → cloud engineer → TAM → pre-sales architect → Google pre-sales (data, migrations, mostly AI now). Fundamentals first: one OS deeply, one language well, then understand how a cloud's 150+ services fit together in designs.
Agents are now basic literacy"It's becoming fundamental, just like understanding any language." Learn how agents are built and hosted (Bedrock on AWS, Vertex AI on Google), pick one or two that speed up your own work, then go deeper into building an end-to-end pipeline for one use case. Biggest wins are on repetitive, templatable work, mainframe modernization included.
Google's 20% projectsTeams post small projects; anyone with spare time can join and get real experience in another role (PM, program manager…) without leaving their job. Same idea anywhere: find gaps (maternity or paternity leave, side projects) to get 1–2 months of hands-on time in the role you want before switching. "If you move into a role without understanding it completely… your old skill set will go waste."
Manager & team filterHis main filter when moving: the manager and team. Ask for a coffee chat even for internal moves at Amazon. People overlook it for tech and package, but at mid-level it decides your experience.
Roles and levels
Role mappingAWS ProServe ≈ Google Cloud Consulting (cloud architect, hands-on). Customer Engineer = pre-sales; you guide the customer without hands-on access, like an account Solutions Architect at AWS.
Levels"It's always level minus one from Amazon to Google." AWS L5 = Google L4. Six years is not Google L5. Google levels are not public and don't appear in job descriptions; you only learn your level at offer stage.
Agents for migration at GoogleHe hasn't worked on external migration agents; internal employee agents exist, similar to AWS's.
Interview loop (customer-facing roles)
1. Recruiter screenChecks suitability. After shortlisting, the recruiter sends topics and a schedule.
2. Role-Related Knowledge (RRK)Usually 1 round (2 at higher levels). Tests depth in your chosen area: compute, data or migrations.
3. PresentationYou present a solution and handle the interviewers' objections. Tests communication, objection handling and sensitive situations.
4. Googleyness & LeadershipLike Amazon's leadership principles. Hypothetical ("what would you do if…") and past-behavior ("tell me about a time you failed / handled an objection").
How to answerSTAR (situation, task, action, result). Always take 1–2 minutes with pen and paper to note keywords before answering. State your assumptions or ask probing questions; don't just jump in. Not every design must be highly available. Some customers need the most cost-effective option, like certification questions. Use the Well-Architected pillars as a checklist.
